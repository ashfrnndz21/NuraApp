Nura — heart concepts

Five original hearts in the app's tone. None is a fingerprint, a pulse line or a letter cut into a heart. Each one says a specific thing about the product: two people, an aura, a cradle, a voice, a family.

A. Open heart, two halves recommended

A heart drawn as two strokes that meet at the top and never quite close at the bottom. The left half is plum, the right is the aura. Two people, the patient and the person who cares for him, and an opening at the bottom because the care is never finished. The dot inside is him. One colour version still reads.

B. Aura heart

Three hearts, one inside the other, fading outward from plum to blush: the person, the family, the system around them. The glow made of the shape itself. Strong on dark backgrounds and as a Lock Screen glyph; the outer ring can animate softly when Nura speaks.

C. Cradle heart

A soft filled heart in the aura, with the cradle from the first mark inside it: a white bowl holding a plum dot. Heart as the shelter, cradle as the holding. The most illustrative of the five; good for the App Store tile and onboarding, a little heavy at favicon size.

D. Speaking heart

A heart that is also a speech bubble, with a small tail and three dots: Nura speaks, on WhatsApp and in voice notes. It says "someone is about to say something kind". Natural as the chat avatar and the notification icon.

E. Held heart

A small aura heart resting in the cradle from the first mark. Someone's heart, held. It is the gentlest of the five and the one that most directly says what a caregiver does. Works as a solid shape for print and stickers.

Recommendation. A, the open heart of two halves. It carries the most meaning with the fewest parts, it is a stroke rather than a fill so it sits lightly on the glass and paper surfaces of the app, and it survives one colour and 16 pixels. Use B's fading rings as the "Nura is speaking" animation and D as the WhatsApp avatar, so the hearts stay one family. E is the gentlest if you want the cradle idea carried through from the first family of marks.
